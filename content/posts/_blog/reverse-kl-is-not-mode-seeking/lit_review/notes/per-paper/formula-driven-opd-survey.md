## Scope verdict

IN SCOPE as a *secondary* source only. It is a survey: no runs, no numbers of its own. It is in scope because §3.2, §3.3, §4.1, §4.3, §6.4, §7.1, §9 and App. C treat "rollouts generated by an old policy and reused", cached/stale log-ratio rewards, and asynchronous generation as first-class taxonomy variables, and because Eqs. 24–27 / 51–52 write the stale-rollout OPD estimator out explicitly. Teacher is assumed fixed/detached throughout ("q(·|c_t) is treated as detached feedback unless a method explicitly trains a co-evolving teacher", §3.3); co-evolving teachers are flagged as a separate case (COPD, Gu et al. 2026) and are out of our sweep's scope.

## Tier

- **D** — the paper's contribution in this sweep is quotable statements. It states the off-policy condition for reused rollouts (§4.1), the bias list for cached rewards (§4.3), the auditing requirement ("rollout policy, teacher freshness", Table 5), and the composability veto on "stale rewards without correction" (§9). All are E3 = survey synthesis by the author's own evidence-tier convention (Table 1, §3.5).
- **Not A.** It never compares distribution-level (full-vocab / top-k) reverse KL against the sampled-token surrogate *under staleness*. See "Tier-A check" below for the near-miss asymmetry, which is structural (which variables each route must report) rather than a robustness claim.
- **Not B/C** — no experiments, so no sync/async numbers anywhere.
- **Not E** — not a production tech report (it cites them: Qwen3, MiMo-v2-Flash, GLM-5, DeepSeek-V4, §8).
- **Not F/G** — it does not run async OPD; it taxonomizes it.

## Setting

- No students, no teachers, no benchmarks, no framework runs. Survey of OPD as a "feedback-to-update problem" (§1), organized from two formula routes: direct distributional loss (Eq. 2) and policy-gradient log-ratio (Eqs. 9–10).
- Teacher fixed: **yes** by convention (§3.3 "detached feedback").
- Convention check: the paper's reverse KL matches ours. Eq. 3 `J_x(θ) = D_KL(π_θ(·|x) ‖ q(·|x))` with π_θ the trained student and q the teacher/target — i.e. KL(student ‖ teacher). Reward `r_t = log q − log π_θ` is positive when the teacher likes the sampled token more than the student does (§3.2, after Eq. 5).
- Frameworks discussed as taxonomy checks (§8.5): SWIFT/Megatron-SWIFT GKD, verl OPD, TRL GKDTrainer + DistillationTrainer, KDFlow, Tinker.

## Async mechanism

Not implemented — *specified*. The survey names three distinct stale objects and keeps them apart:

1. **Stale rollout policy** (the states): "the rollout policy used to collect actions, such as current, cached, replayed, or importance-corrected policies" (§3.2 "Degrees of freedom"). In the Table 3 taxonomy the State/rollout-source variable ranges over "Teacher-forced states, student rollouts, mixed data/rollout states, recent-policy replay, or environment states", with main risk "Exposure gap or flawed-state trap".
2. **Stale cached rewards** (the log-ratios): "PG-style OPD must distinguish rewards computed from the current student from cached rewards computed under an old policy. Using current log-ratios, stale cached log-ratios, or importance-corrected log-ratios defines different estimators and different bias terms." (§3.3).
3. **Stale teacher / cache** (the scores themselves): Table 6 row "Cached-teacher inconsistency … Cached log-probabilities can become inconsistent with the current student, prompt template, tokenizer, or rollout distribution."

Staleness is never quantified: no step lag, no buffer size, no mixing ratio, no off-policyness metric with a value. The only *measurement* asks are diagnostics: "stale-policy drift" as a required report item (§4.5), and "teacher-cache consistency or stale-score drift for cached or near-policy systems" (§7.1). Table 7 gives the cache-integrity row: diagnostics "Teacher-cache consistency; prompt-template versioning; tokenizer and scoring replay"; warning "Cached scores drift from re-scoring, teacher checkpoints or prompt templates change, or token events no longer match stored scores"; response "Version caches, shorten cache windows, re-score held-out slices, freeze the teacher interface, or refresh stale rollouts."

## Objective

Both routes are given. Vocabulary mapping to our terms:

**Direct-loss route** (`rkl` / `fkl` / top-k, Eq. 2, §3.1) — the prefix law `d` is explicitly allowed to be a replay distribution:

```latex
\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{direct}}=\mathbb{E}_{c_{t}\sim d}\left[w_{t}D_{\Omega_{t}}\left[\pi_{\theta}(\cdot|c_{t}),q(\cdot|c_{t})\right]\right], \tag{2}
```

with the full-vocabulary local reverse KL (Eq. 6):

```latex
D_{t}^{\mathrm{RKL}}=D_{\mathrm{KL}}(\pi_{\theta}(\cdot|c_{t})\|q(\cdot|c_{t}))=\sum_{v\in\mathcal{V}}\pi_{\theta}(v|c_{t})\log\frac{\pi_{\theta}(v|c_{t})}{q(v|c_{t})}. \tag{6}
```

**PG route** (`rkl_pg`, Eqs. 5/11, 9, 10, §3.2):

```latex
r_{t}=\log q(y_{t}|c_{t})-\log\pi_{\theta}(y_{t}|c_{t}), \tag{5, restated as 11}
\Delta\theta_{\mathrm{OPD}}\propto\sum_{t}A_{t}\nabla_{\theta}\log\pi_{\theta}(y_{t}|c_{t}), \tag{9}
\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{PG}}=-\sum_{t}\operatorname{sg}(A_{t})\log\pi_{\theta}(y_{t}|c_{t}), \tag{10}
```

and the local score identity linking the two (Eq. 7), which is the paper's statement that one sampled token is an unbiased estimator of the *fixed-prefix* reverse-KL direction:

```latex
-\nabla_{\theta}D_{t}^{\mathrm{RKL}}=\mathbb{E}_{t}\left[\left(\rho_{t}(a)-1\right)g_{t}(a)\right]=\mathbb{E}_{t}\left[\rho_{t}(a)g_{t}(a)\right],\quad \mathbb{E}_{t}[g_{t}(a)]=0. \tag{7}
```

Sequence-level return-to-go (Eq. 15) and the immediate-token surrogate (Eq. 16), the pair whose staleness status §4.1 rules on:

```latex
\hat{g}_{\mathrm{seq}}=\sum_{t=1}^{T}G_{t}s_{t},\qquad G_{t}=\sum_{k=t}^{T}r_{k}. \tag{15}
\hat{g}_{\mathrm{tok}}=\sum_{t=1}^{T}r_{t}s_{t}. \tag{16}
```

## Off-policy correction

Named, never written as an equation. The corrections the survey requires are "importance correction, PPO-style clipping (Schulman et al., 2017), or a trust-region approximation (Schulman et al., 2015)" (§4.1) and "PPO-style ratios against π_θold" (§4.3). **No importance-ratio formula, no clip value, no truncation threshold, no mask rule appears anywhere in the paper.**

The old-policy estimator itself *is* written out (§4.3, the passage the sweep asked for). Verbatim LaTeX from the HTML:

```latex
r_{k}^{\mathrm{old}}=\log q(y_{k}|c_{k})-\log\pi_{\theta_{\mathrm{old}}}(y_{k}|c_{k}) \tag{24}
V_{\phi}(c_{t})\approx\mathbb{E}_{y_{t:T}\sim\pi_{\theta_{\mathrm{old}}}(\cdot|c_{t})}\left[\sum_{k=t}^{T}r_{k}^{\mathrm{old}}\mid c_{t}\right]. \tag{25}
\delta_{t}=r_{t}^{\mathrm{old}}+V_{\phi}(c_{t+1})-V_{\phi}(c_{t}), \tag{26}
\hat{A}_{t}^{\mathrm{GAE}}=\sum_{l=0}^{T-t}\lambda^{l}\delta_{t+l}. \tag{27}
```

with the actor loss given inline right after Eq. 27: `-\sum_{t}\operatorname{sg}(\hat{A}_{t}^{\mathrm{GAE}})\log\pi_{\theta}(y_{t}|c_{t})`, "optionally with PPO-style ratios against π_θold". App. C repeats the same two equations as (51) and (52) (identical LaTeX, (52) ends with a comma) and adds the flat verdict: "This is off-policy for the current student unless the update uses importance correction, PPO-style ratios (Schulman et al., 2017), or a trust-region approximation (Schulman et al., 2015)."

For contrast, the on-policy GAE-OPD hypothesis is Eqs. 21–23 (`V^π`, `δ^π_t`, `\hat{A}_{t}^{\lambda}=\sum_{l=0}^{T-t}\lambda^{l}\delta_{t+l}^{\pi}`), i.e. Eqs. 24–27 are exactly Eqs. 21–23 with π_θ replaced by π_θold and V^π by V_φ.

## Explanation offered

The mechanism identified is **estimator bias from distribution shift between the rollout policy and the current student**, kept strictly separate from the paper's other two axes (temporal-credit bias from discounting/truncation, and vocabulary-routing bias from support truncation).

- §4.1, immediately after Eq. 15: "If rollouts are generated by an old policy and reused for multiple updates, Eq. 15 becomes off-policy unless importance correction, PPO-style clipping (Schulman et al., 2017), or a trust-region approximation (Schulman et al., 2015) is added."
- §4.3, the four-item list of what breaks the expectation-preserving property: "Cached old-policy rewards, repeated actor updates, missing importance correction, or trust-region approximations introduce off-policy bias." (Note "repeated actor updates" — multiple epochs over one rollout batch is counted as a staleness source in its own right.)
- §4.3 conclusion, the sentence the sweep was sent to verify: "Without small policy drift, importance correction, PPO-style ratios, or a trust-region approximation (Schulman et al., 2015), this old-policy surrogate should not be described as the exact current-student sequence reverse-KL gradient." (verbatim, confirmed in both PDF and HTML)
- Fig. 4 caption ties the critic and the staleness together: "GAE-OPD uses value-based TD residuals whose practical bias depends on critic error and policy staleness."
- §6.4 is the only place a *systems* explanation is offered, and it is attributed, not derived: "Cached-teacher systems make teacher dynamics part of the objective. Lightning OPD reduces cost by precomputing teacher log-probabilities, but its teacher-consistency condition shows why a stale teacher, prompt template, tokenizer, or rollout source changes the meaning of the stored reward (Wu et al., 2026). Near-policy and online variants make the same issue operational: asynchronous generation and teacher lag can improve throughput only if stale feedback remains close enough to the intended on-policy interface (Rang et al., 2026; Ye et al., 2026a)."

### Tier-A check: is distribution-level RKL claimed more/less robust to staleness?

**No such claim is made.** The paper never puts "full-vocabulary / top-k reverse KL" and "sampled-token log-ratio" side by side under a staleness condition. The comparisons it *does* make between the two routes are about a different axis — horizon bias and variance, on-policy:

- §3.2: the sampled-token update "is an unbiased one-sample estimator of the fixed-prefix local reverse-KL descent direction, while it is generally a biased surrogate for the sequence-level reverse-KL direction because it omits future log-ratio terms."
- §4.1, attributed to Fu et al. 2026: "under bounded per-token log-ratio and score terms, token-local sums have a much tighter worst-case horizon dependence than sequence-level estimators, with simplified bounds of O(T^2) versus O(T^4)".
- §5.5: "PG-style OPD can have high horizon variance; direct top-k losses can introduce truncation bias".

The closest thing to a tier-A asymmetry is **structural and ours to read off, not asserted** (flagging it as our inference, E3-style):

- §3.1: "We use d as the rollout or replay distribution from which prefixes are collected; unless stated otherwise, direct-loss updates differentiate the local distributional objective conditional on these prefixes, not the sampling process that produced them. When the prefixes are generated by the current student but treated as fixed training data, direct-loss OPD is a semi-gradient with respect to the full on-policy objective."
- §3.3: "direct-loss OPD on student rollouts is usually a local semi-gradient: the collected prefixes are fixed data for the update, even if a current or recent student generated them."
- Table 5 "Must report" columns: the direct-loss row asks for "Support source and size, normalization convention, divergence direction, masks or weights, detached prefixes/targets, and drift regularizer" — **no rollout-policy or teacher-freshness entry**; the PG row asks for "Rollout policy, teacher freshness, reward definition, horizon, baseline/control variate, stop-gradient convention, clipping or trust region, and negative-advantage routing."

So the survey's own auditing checklist treats staleness as a PG-route variable and not a direct-loss-route variable, and its only statement about replayed prefixes in the direct-loss route is that they make the update a semi-gradient (a statement about the state distribution d, not about the per-prefix gradient being wrong). That is consistent with the tier-A hypothesis but is not an argument for it, and the paper offers no bound, no variance statement, and no experiment on the question.

## Not in the source

- No experiments of any kind, therefore no async-vs-sync numbers, no throughput numbers, no staleness sweep.
- No quantified staleness: no lag in steps, no buffer size, no fresh/stale mixing ratio, no numeric off-policyness threshold. "Close enough to the intended on-policy interface" (§6.4) is never made precise.
- No importance-ratio, clipping, or trust-region **equation** — corrections are only named (§4.1, §4.3, App. C).
- No comparison of direct-loss (full-vocab / top-k) vs PG (sampled-token) robustness **under staleness** — the route comparison is about horizon bias and variance on-policy (§3.2, §4.1, §5.5). The tier-A question is left open.
- No analysis of the direct-loss route's own off-policy status beyond the one-line "semi-gradient" remark (§3.1, §3.3): no statement about what replaying prefixes does to E_{c_t∼d}, no correction proposed for d.
- GAE-OPD (Eqs. 21–27) is explicitly a **hypothesis, never implemented**: "GAE-OPD and CR-OPD remain hypotheses generated by those variables rather than reported implementations" (§5.6); evidence level E3 (Table 1, §3.5).
- The Lightning OPD and Near-Policy claims in §6.4 are one-sentence attributions with no numbers, so they cannot substitute for reading those primaries (both already noted separately for Lightning OPD).
