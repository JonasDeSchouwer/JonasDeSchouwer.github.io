## Tier

- **A (qualified).** §3.3.5 "Logit matching" is a stated *argument* that the sampled-token estimator is more robust than distribution-level (teacher-top-k or full-vocab) matching, backed by preliminary experiments. **Direction: sampled-token > distribution-level.** Caveat that matters for our sweep: the mechanism invoked is *student-prefix / teacher-support mismatch* ("prefixes … have limited support under the teacher distribution"), **not** behavior-policy staleness. The report never argues that either estimator tolerates *stale rollouts* better. Treat as tier A only for the support-mismatch axis.
- **D.** Many quotable statements about asynchronous OPD: the pipeline description (§3.3.1), the "one-step off-policy asynchronous RL setup" for both RL and MOPD (§3.6.1), and the async-design open problem (§3.3.5, 3rd bullet).
- **E.** Production tech report for a 550B-A55B open-weights model whose last *policy-training* stage is asynchronous MOPD (§3, Fig. 9; only head-only MTP Boosting, §3.4, follows it). The abstract lists MOPD as a headline technology.
- **G.** Async OPD is used but is not itself the subject of study — no async ablation anywhere.
- **Not B, not C.** No async-vs-sync numbers of any kind (verified by exhaustive grep for sync/async/stale/off-policy: 14 hits, none comparative).
- **Not F.** MOPD is a main point; its *asynchrony* is an implementation detail, never varied.

## Setting

- **Student:** Nemotron 3 Ultra 550B total / 55B active, MoE hybrid Mamba-Attention (Table 1: 108 layers, d=8192, 512 experts/layer top-22, 2 shared-weight MTP layers), pretrained on 20T tokens in NVFP4, context extended to 1M. MOPD starts from the **RLVR checkpoint** (SFT → unified RLVR → MOPD warmup → MOPD), Fig. 9.
- **Teachers:** "more than ten specialized teacher models, each optimized through its own domain-specific training pipeline" (§3.3). Fig. 10 names them: STEM, Chat, Instruction-following, Terminal-use, Conversational tool-use, SWE, Search, Office work, Usability, Agentic Safety, Coding (+ "RLVR Student also serves as a self-teacher for areas not covered by specialized teachers", Fig. 10 caption). Nearly all are initialized from Ultra checkpoints (§3.3.2).
- **Teacher fixed?** **Yes within a MOPD run**; teachers appear only as scoring models π^{T_i} in Eqs. 1–3, with gradients only through π_θ. But there is an **outer loop**: "after obtaining an MOPD-trained checkpoint, we branch out new rounds of teacher training initialized from the updated student model and subsequently merge the resulting improvements back into the next MOPD stage. This iterative co-evolution between student and teachers …" (§3.3) — two iterations (MOPD1, MOPD2). In-scope for the sweep (each iteration is fixed-teacher), but flag the co-evolution.
- **Domain/benchmarks:** agentic (Terminal Bench 2.0, GDPVal, SWE-Bench Verified, TauBench Telecom, BrowseComp), reasoning (LiveCodeBench v6, IMOAnswerBench, HLE), factuality/IF (OmniScience Non-Hallucination, IFBench, Multi-Challenge). Table 5.
- **Framework/code:** NeMo-RL on GB200 NVL72 with Slurm + Ray + vLLM generation + Megatron policy workers (§3.6.2); recipes at `https://github.com/NVIDIA-NeMo/Nemotron`. Weights, data and RL environments open-sourced; MOPD training code itself is not named in the report.
- **Prior art it builds on:** "Building on prior work on on-policy distillation (Yang et al., 2026; Lu and Lab, 2025; Xiao et al., 2026)" (§3.3.1) = Nemotron-Cascade 2 (2603.19220), Thinking Machines' *On-policy distillation* blog, MiMo-V2-Flash (2601.02780).

## Async mechanism

**What is stale:** the *behavior* student that produced the rollout, versus the *current* learner student. Teachers are not stale.

> "In our implementation, MOPD is executed asynchronously. Rollout workers, teacher-scoring workers, and learner workers run in a pipeline. A trajectory may therefore be generated by a stale behavior policy π_behav, while the learner optimizes a newer student snapshot. To stabilize this setting, we decouple the behavior policy from the proximal policy π_prox used as the trust-region center (Fu et al., 2026)." (§3.3.1)

> "To maximize efficiency, MOPD is conducted asynchronously, with student rollout generation, teacher scoring, and student optimization fully pipelined." (§3.3)

**Three distinct student policies** appear in the update: π_behav (stale sampler), π_prox (trust-region centre / recent learner snapshot), π_θ (current learner). Nothing in the report says how π_prox is refreshed relative to π_behav or π_θ.

**How staleness is measured — the only quantitative statement is in the infra section:**

> "During RL and MOPD, we train using a one-step off-policy asynchronous RL setup, so rollout generation is overlapped with the policy update, and the step time is bounded by whichever stage is slower." (§3.6.1)

So **staleness = one optimizer step, and it is the SAME for MOPD data as for RLVR data** — the report explicitly covers both with one sentence. No replay buffer, no mixing ratio, no "off-policyness" metric, no staleness sweep. The range of staleness studied is a single point (1 step).

**The "192K" figure is NOT staleness.** It is the maximum *generation length* of a MOPD rollout:

> "MOPD training uses a maximum generation length of 192K tokens, matching the longest generation length used across all teacher training runs. Each training batch contains 1,024 prompts, with one rollout per prompt. In our ablation studies, using multiple rollouts did not yield additional benefits." (§3.3.1, final paragraph)

(The same 192K appears in §3.3.2 for the SWE teacher's own RL: "Our end-to-end RL was conducted using 192K generation length and a maximum of 200 agent turns.") Any reading of "192K" as tokens-behind or steps-behind is unsupported by the source.

**MOPD batch vs RL batch (for contrast):** MOPD = 1,024 prompts × **1** rollout, 192K max gen (§3.3.1). RLVR = "global batch size of 8192, with each sample generating 16 rollouts … maximum generation length of 48K tokens, which is later increased to 64K tokens" (§3.2), following "the asynchronous GRPO algorithm with the stability optimizations proposed in NVIDIA (2026)" [Nemotron 3 Super].

**Rollout shape:** "In practice, we use single-turn rollouts, similar to PivotRL (Yi et al., 2026), for most of the agentic tasks." (§3.3.5)

## Objective

`rkl_pg` — sampled-token score-function/advantage form of reverse KL, in a PPO-clipped update. Direction matches our convention (student ‖ teacher), stated explicitly.

**Eq. 1 (idealized fully on-policy objective, maximized):**

```latex
\mathcal{J}_{\mathrm{MOPD}}(\theta)=\sum_{i=1}^{N}\lambda_{i}\mathbb{E}_{q\sim\mathcal{D}_{i},\;y\sim\pi_{\theta}(\cdot|q)}\left[\sum_{t=1}^{H}\log\pi^{T_{i}}(y_{t}|s_{t})-\log\pi_{\theta}(y_{t}|s_{t})\right]
```

Surrounding prose: "MOPD trains the student to match the corresponding teacher on states induced by the student itself. In the fully on-policy case, this corresponds to maximizing the negative reverse-KL objective" … "where λ_i controls the sampling or loss weight of domain i. Equivalently, at each prefix s_t, the student minimizes D_KL(π_θ(·|s_t)‖π^{T_i}(·|s_t)). Thus, unlike mixed-environment RLVR, where the reward is typically sparse and environment dependent, MOPD provides a dense token-level learning signal from the relevant teacher distribution." (§3.3.1)

**Per-token logs (unnumbered display, §3.3.1):** ℓ^behav_t = log π_behav(y_t|s_t), ℓ^prox_t = log π_prox(y_t|s_t), ℓ^{T_i}_t = log π^{T_i}(y_t|s_t).

**Eq. 2 (the advantage) — "The dense distillation advantage is the sampled negative reverse-KL estimate with respect to the proximal policy:"**

```latex
\widehat{A}_{t}=\mathrm{sg}\!\left[\ell^{T_{i}}_{t}-\ell^{\mathrm{prox}}_{t}\right]
```

"where sg[·] denotes stop-gradient."

Note the baseline is **π_prox, not π_θ and not π_behav** — the advantage is teacher-minus-trust-region-centre, so the reverse-KL estimate itself is made robust to rollout staleness by being computed against the recent learner snapshot rather than the sampler.

**Eq. 3 (what is actually maximized):**

```latex
\mathcal{J}_{\mathrm{async\text{-}MOPD}}(\theta)=\mathbb{E}_{q\sim\mathcal{D}_{i},\;y\sim\pi_{\mathrm{behav}}}\left[\sum_{t=1}^{H}m_{t}c_{t}\min\!\left(r_{t}(\theta)\widehat{A}_{t},\,\mathrm{clip}\!\left(r_{t}(\theta),1-\epsilon,1+\epsilon\right)\widehat{A}_{t}\right)\right]
```

"where m_t represents token-level masking using IcePop strategy (Team et al., 2025)."

Not stated anywhere: λ_i values, learning rate, number of MOPD steps, sampling temperature, length normalization, loss aggregation (token-mean vs sequence-mean), the value of ε.

## Off-policy correction

Three separate mechanisms, all in Eq. 3 / its preamble (§3.3.1):

1. **Behavior→proximal importance ratio (stop-gradiented, un-clipped, multiplicative on the whole term):**

```latex
c_{t}=\mathrm{sg}\!\left[\frac{\pi_{\mathrm{prox}}(y_{t}|s_{t})}{\pi_{\mathrm{behav}}(y_{t}|s_{t})}\right]
```

> "Here, c_t accounts for the mismatch between the stale rollout policy π_behav and the proximal learner policy π_prox" (§3.3.1)

Per-token, not per-sequence. **No truncation or clamp is stated for c_t** (contrast Nemotron-Cascade 2, which truncates its single ratio to [0.5, 2.0], and the NeMo-RL `mopd.yaml` which uses TIS clamp [0.2, 5]).

2. **Proximal→current policy ratio with PPO clipping:**

```latex
r_{t}(\theta)=\frac{\pi_{\theta}(y_{t}|s_{t})}{\pi_{\mathrm{prox}}(y_{t}|s_{t})}
```

> "while r_t(θ) is the policy ratio optimized by the learner. PPO-style clipping is applied to r_t(θ) around the proximal policy π_prox." (§3.3.1)

Standard `min(r·A, clip(r, 1−ε, 1+ε)·A)`. **ε is never given a numeric value anywhere in the report.**

3. **IcePop token mask m_t.** Cited to "Team et al., 2025" = Ling Team et al., *Every step evolves: Scaling reinforcement learning for trillion-scale thinking model*, arXiv 2510.18855 (Ring-1T) — see bibliography. **The report gives no definition of IcePop and no thresholds** — the single sentence after Eq. 3 is the entirety of it. (For our own reference: IcePop as defined by Ring-1T zeroes tokens whose training/inference log-prob discrepancy leaves a band; the NeMo-RL `ClippedPGLoss` IcePop mode documents reference bounds 0.5–5. Neither the band nor the bounds are in this report.)

The decoupled π_prox trust-region construction is credited to **Fu et al., 2026** = the AReaL asynchronous-RL system (Wei Fu, Jiaxuan Gao, et al., *AReaL: A large-scale asynchronous reinforcement learning system for language reasoning*, NeurIPS 38:36256–36282, 2026).

## Explanation offered

**(a) Why sampled-token rather than distribution-level (the §3.3.5 negative result) — support mismatch + teacher-logit noise on off-support prefixes:**

> "We hypothesize that full-distribution matching may impose an overly strong local constraint on prefixes sampled from the student policy, particularly when those prefixes have limited support under the teacher distribution. In this regime, teacher logits can become poorly calibrated or less informative, and matching the full distribution may amplify noise from off-support states. By contrast, the sampled-token objective applies supervision only to realized actions and may therefore provide a more stable on-policy learning signal." (§3.3.5, "Logit matching")

Mechanism: **teacher-side support mismatch / teacher inconsistency off-support**, amplified by the full-distribution integrand. Note the argument is about *student-vs-teacher* mismatch on student-induced prefixes; it is **not** an argument about behavior-policy staleness, and the report never connects the two.

**(b) Why MOPD works where it works, and fails on HLE — the same support argument:**

> "these results indicate that MOPD is particularly effective when the teacher's advantage can be expressed as token-level preferences over trajectories that the student is already able to sample, such as tool-use decisions, environment interactions, abstention behavior, and multi-step execution patterns." (§3.3.4)

> "Since MOPD scores student-generated trajectories, its learning signal is strongest when those trajectories lie within the teacher's support. When the missing capability requires reasoning paths that the student rarely samples, student rollouts become effectively out-of-distribution for the teacher, making the token-level supervision less informative." (§3.3.4)

**(c) Why the warmup exists — the same mechanism, acted on:**

> "We hypothesize that when the teacher and student are trained on different SFT data, they acquire different reasoning behaviors and induce different output distributions. This distribution mismatch can cause student-generated trajectories to be out-of-distribution for the teacher, reducing the quality and reliability of the supervision signals provided by the teacher." (§3.3.3)

> "This improves the reliability of teacher supervision by increasing the likelihood that student-generated trajectories remain within the teacher's support." (§3.3.3)

> "Warmup substantially improves MOPD in agentic domains, where increasing overlap between student rollouts and teacher-supported trajectories makes teacher scoring more informative. In contrast, warmup has little effect on HLE …" (§3.3.4)

**(d) Why the async update is built the way it is** — stated as a design choice, not an analysis: c_t "accounts for the mismatch between the stale rollout policy π_behav and the proximal learner policy π_prox" and clipping is "around the proximal policy π_prox" (§3.3.1), citing AReaL (Fu et al., 2026). No variance analysis, no bias discussion, no derivation.

Full §3.3.5 "Logit matching" bullet, verbatim (the distribution-level negative result):

> "**Logit matching.** A natural alternative to the sampled-token objective is distribution-level distillation, where the student is trained to match the teacher's predictive distribution over the top-k tokens or the full vocabulary at each prefix. In our preliminary experiments, these objectives did not improve MOPD performance and consistently underperformed the sampled-token objective on some agentic benchmarks such as Terminal Bench. We hypothesize that full-distribution matching may impose an overly strong local constraint on prefixes sampled from the student policy, particularly when those prefixes have limited support under the teacher distribution. In this regime, teacher logits can become poorly calibrated or less informative, and matching the full distribution may amplify noise from off-support states. By contrast, the sampled-token objective applies supervision only to realized actions and may therefore provide a more stable on-policy learning signal. Characterizing when broader distributional supervision is beneficial remains an open problem."

Framing caveat for §3.3.5: **whose top-k is never stated.** The text says "match the teacher's predictive distribution over the top-k tokens or the full vocabulary" — the tokens are the teacher's *predictive distribution's* support, but whether the top-k index set is taken from the teacher, the student, or their union is not specified; k is not given; the divergence used for the logit-matching variant (forward KL? reverse KL? MSE on logits?) is **not stated either**. The only certain reading: it is distribution-level (teacher-top-k or full-vocab) matching on student-sampled prefixes, and it lost to the sampled-token estimator on some agentic benchmarks including Terminal Bench.

Also relevant, the preamble that scopes the whole §3.3.5 list:

> "During the development of MOPD, we evaluated several technically plausible variants that did not improve performance under our current experimental setup. We share these observations to make the empirical picture more complete, but they should not be interpreted as evidence that these approaches are fundamentally ineffective. With further research, we believe these directions could yield MOPD approaches that outperform our current setup, and we encourage the community to continue exploring them." (§3.3.5)

## Not in the source

- **No MOPD hyperparameters** beyond batch (1,024 prompts × 1 rollout) and 192K max generation length: no learning rate, no schedule, no step count, no λ_i, no sampling temperature, no length normalization / loss-aggregation rule, no KL-to-reference penalty.
- **No statement of how π_prox is produced or refreshed** (every N steps? previous learner snapshot? same as π_behav shifted by one?).

- **No link from the sampled-token choice to staleness.** The report's robustness argument for sampled-token is about *teacher support*, never about behavior-policy lag; our sweep's core question (does distribution-level RKL tolerate staleness better or worse than rkl_pg?) is not addressed.
- **No MOPD training code or MOPD appendix** in this report (Appendix A = benchmark details + harness robustness).
