## Tier

- **None for staleness.** "Off-policy" in this paper means **teacher-generated prefixes** ("Off-Policy Draft from Teacher", Figure 2; §4.3 "an off-policy prefix `x[:l]` generated by the teacher, followed by an on-policy continuation `x[l:]` generated by the student"). The student never trains on rollouts from an earlier version of itself. The words "stale", "replay", "asynchronous", "old policy", "behavior policy", "epoch", "mini-batch" and "buffer" do not occur anywhere in v3. By the sweep rule (teacher-generated data is out of scope) this is tier **none**.
- **D (weak, adjacent)** — one genuinely relevant design rule: **the KL direction is chosen to match the sampling distribution, so that a K1 single-sample estimator stays valid and no importance ratio is ever needed**. On student samples it uses K1 reverse KL; on teacher samples it switches to K1 forward KL: "For the off-policy region, since samples are generated from the teacher, `x ~ pi_T`, we adopt the K1 estimator for the forward KL ... which achieves O(n) memory complexity." (§4.3). That is an estimator-robustness statement about off-policy *samples*, just with the teacher rather than a stale student as the behavior policy.
- **D (weak, second strand)** — a truncation-bias result relevant to which estimator survives a distribution mismatch: standalone teacher-top-k forward KL **collapses to ~0** on student-sampled tokens (Table 1: AIME24 0.00, AIME25 0.00, AMC23 4.21) while K1 reverse KL trains fine (46.79 avg), with the explanation quoted below. Directionally the opposite of "FKL is the robust one".
- **Not A** — the only robustness argument is about teacher-vs-student mismatch at a *fresh* prefix, never about current-vs-earlier student. **Not B / C** — no sync-vs-async numbers. **Not E / F / G** — no async system anywhere.
- Direction convention matches ours: "RKL" = KL(pi_S ‖ pi_T) with the student `pi_S` first (Eq. 1–2, §3.1).
- **Note for the annealed-guidance question:** TrOPD's cosine anneal of the teacher-prefix length to zero (§4.3) is the closest thing in this cluster to an "off-policy cold start"; that phrase does **not** appear in Fu et al. 2026 (arXiv 2603.25562), which is where the task brief expected it.

## Setting

- Students: DeepSeek-R1-Distill-Qwen-1.5B (single- and multi-domain) and Qwen3-SFT-1.7B (multi-domain; "The student model is trained from Qwen3-1.7B-Base using the same SFT recipe", §5.1).
- Teachers, all **fixed**: Skywork-OR1-Math-7B (single-domain math), Skywork-OR1-7B (multi-domain), Qwen3-Nemotron-4B (multi-domain with the 1.7B student; trained by the authors from Qwen3-4B-Base via SFT+RLVR, App. A, *before* distillation, and frozen during OPD).
- Data: OpenThoughts3 prompts (math only for single-domain; math+code+science for multi-domain). "Only the prompts are retained for training." (§5.1).
- Benchmarks: AIME24, AIME25, AMC23 (avg of 32 evaluations), LiveCodeBench v6, GPQA Diamond, MMLU-Redux v2, IFBench.
- Training (§5.1): 200 OPD steps, fixed lr 5e-6, top-k support k=64 for all FKL terms, beta=0.001 for off-policy guidance, prompt batch 128 x 4 rollouts, max generation 8096 tokens. Framework not named; "The project homepage is available at GitHub" (Abstract).
- Teacher's own RL (App. A, not part of OPD): "During RLVR, GRPO is employed with a group size of 16, together with masked importance sampling to improve consistency between training and inference. The batch size is set to 128, and model parameters are updated every 2048 rollouts." — 128 x 16 = 2048, i.e. one update per rollout batch, fully on-policy.

## Async mechanism

**None.** Every student-sampled token in the objective comes from the current student. What the paper calls off-policy is a **teacher-written prefix**, refreshed by a schedule, not stale student data:

- What is "off-policy": "The distillation trajectory consists of two parts: an off-policy prefix `x[:l]` generated by the teacher, followed by an on-policy continuation `x[l:]` generated by the student. For complex reasoning tasks, this design avoids low-quality outputs caused by the limited capability of the student model." (§4.3).
- Schedule (the only "staleness-like" knob, and it is about teacher-prefix length, not lag): "Initially, the maximum off-policy trajectory length is set to the maximum training sequence length. During training, it is gradually annealed to zero using a cosine schedule, such that generation becomes fully on-policy by the end of training." (§4.3). Range: `l` from the full 8096-token budget down to 0 over 200 steps; no intermediate value is ablated and no per-step value is tabulated.
- Staleness measured: not at all. The only ratio tracked is teacher/student at the same fresh prefix (Eq. 6), never current/old student.
- Updates per rollout batch: not stated. No `ppo_epochs`, no mini-batch size; nothing implies reuse.

## Objective

Mixed: sampled-token reverse KL inside the trust region, teacher-top-k forward KL outside it, teacher-prefix forward KL for guidance. In our vocabulary: **`rkl_pg` (K1) on trusted student tokens + `fkl` truncated to teacher-top-64 on outlier student tokens + `fkl` (K1, sampled) on teacher-prefix tokens**.

- Full-vocab and K1 references, Eq. (1) and Eq. (2) (§3.2):
  `\mathcal{J}^{\mathrm{KD}}=-\mathrm{RKL}(\pi_{S}\parallel\pi_{T})=-\sum_{x\in\mathcal{V}}\pi_{S}\log\frac{\pi_{S}}{\pi_{T}}.`
  `\mathcal{J}^{\mathrm{KD}}=-\mathrm{RKL}(\pi_{S}\parallel\pi_{T})=-\mathbb{E}_{x\sim\pi_{S}}\left[\log\frac{\pi_{S}}{\pi_{T}}\right].`
- Truncated FKL, Eq. (3) (§4.1): `\mathcal{J}_{\mathrm{FKL}}^{\mathrm{top}\text{-}k}=-\sum_{v\in\mathcal{V}^{T}_{k}}\pi_{T,v}\log\frac{\pi_{T,v}}{\pi_{S,v}}.`
- On-policy region split, Eq. (5) (§4.2):
  `\mathcal{J}_{x}^{On}=-\mathbb{M}_{x}\mathrm{KL}(\pi_{S}\parallel\pi_{T})-\overline{\mathbb{M}}_{x}\mathrm{KL}(\pi_{T}\parallel\pi_{S})=-\mathbb{M}_{x}\log\frac{\pi_{S}}{\pi_{T}}-\overline{\mathbb{M}}_{x}\sum_{v\in\mathcal{V}^{T}_{k}}\pi_{T,v}\log\frac{\pi_{T,v}}{\pi_{S,v}}.`
- Off-policy guidance, Eq. (8) (§4.3):
  `\mathcal{J}_{x}=-\beta\mathrm{KL}_{x[:l]\sim\pi_{T}}(\pi_{T}\parallel\pi_{S})+\mathcal{J}^{\mathrm{On}}_{x[l:]}=-\beta\mathbb{I}[x\sim\pi_{T}]\log\frac{\pi_{T}}{\pi_{S}}+\mathbb{I}[x\sim\pi_{S}]\mathcal{J}^{\mathrm{On}}_{x[l:]}`
- Unified objective, Eq. (9) (§4.3):
  `\mathcal{J}_{x}^{\mathrm{TrOPD}}=-\mathbb{I}[x\sim\pi_{S}]\overline{\mathbb{M}}_{x}\sum_{v\in\mathcal{V}^{T}_{k}}\pi_{T,v}\log\frac{\pi_{T,v}}{\pi_{S,v}}-\mathbb{I}[x\sim\pi_{S}]\mathbb{M}_{x}\log\frac{\pi_{S}}{\pi_{T}}-\beta\,\mathbb{I}[x\sim\pi_{T}]\log\frac{\pi_{T}}{\pi_{S}}.`
- Per-region summary, Table 2: on-policy trust region `x~pi_S`, objective `-KL(pi_S‖pi_T)`, estimator `log(pi_T(x)/pi_S(x))`, memory O(n); on-policy outlier `x~pi_S`, objective `-KL(pi_T‖pi_S)`, estimator `sum_{v in V_T^(k)} pi_T(v) log(pi_S(v)/pi_T(v))`, memory O(nk); off-policy guidance `x~pi_T`, objective `-beta KL(pi_T‖pi_S)`, estimator `beta log(pi_S(x)/pi_T(x))`, memory O(n).
- **Sign glitch to flag:** the §4.3 prose writes "KL(pi_T ‖ pi_S) = log pi_S/pi_T", which is the negative of the K1 estimator of that KL under `x ~ pi_T`. Table 2 and Eq. (8)/(9) are internally consistent (the *objective* `J = -beta KL` is estimated by `beta log(pi_S/pi_T)`), so the prose sentence is the sloppy one.

## Off-policy correction

- **No importance ratio anywhere.** The mismatch is handled by *switching the KL direction to match whichever policy drew the sample*, so the K1 estimator is always used on samples from its own outer distribution: reverse-KL K1 on student samples, forward-KL K1 on teacher samples (§4.3, Table 2). No clipping, no truncation, no ratio mask of any kind is applied for the policy mismatch.
- The only ratio in the paper is the **teacher-acceptance trust mask**, Eq. (6) (§4.2), which is `pi_T/pi_S` at the *same fresh prefix* — a supervision-reliability gate, not an off-policyness correction:
  `P_{\mathrm{trust}}(x)=\min\left(\frac{\pi_{T}(x)}{\pi_{S}(x)},1\right).`
  with `\mathbb{M}_x \sim \mathrm{Bernoulli}(P_{\mathrm{trust}}(x))`, motivated by speculative decoding: "This design is motivated by speculative decoding, where the probability that the teacher model agrees with a token decoded by the student model satisfies `P_accept(x) ∝ min(pi_T(x)/pi_S(x), 1)`. By selecting only the decoding regions accepted by the teacher, it can be ensured that the student model remains within effectively supervised regions under the K1 estimator." (§4.2).
- Baselines it replaces, both also fresh-prefix reliability fixes, not IS: REOPOLD reward clipping `R(x)=max(log(pi_S/pi_T), tau)` and Mask Outlier `R(x)=I[R>tau] log(pi_S/pi_T)` (§4.2). No tau value is given.

## Explanation offered

- Why K1 reverse KL breaks under teacher/student mismatch (§3.2, the paper's core mechanism — *support mismatch amplified by the sampled-token estimator*): "When the discrepancy between the teacher and student distributions is large, the teacher may assign extremely low probabilities to trajectories sampled from the student policy, i.e., `E_{x~P_S}[pi_T(x)] ≈ 0`. In such low-confidence regions, the K1-based policy-gradient signal can become extremely negative, i.e., `∇J = (1/pi_S(x)) log(pi_T(x)/pi_S(x)) → -∞`. Therefore, student-generated trajectories that receive extremely low confidence from the teacher induce significant policy-gradient outliers, which destabilize OPD optimization and limit its potential final performance."
- Why the student's own rollouts limit it (§3.2): "Since OPD is optimized exclusively on trajectories sampled from the student policy, the student may struggle to generate high-quality responses for challenging problems. As a result, low-quality SoG trajectories restrict the effective optimization space and prevent the student from receiving informative supervision from higher-quality responses." — this is the motivation for pulling in teacher prefixes.
- Why truncated FKL cannot be the standalone estimator on student samples (§4.1): "stand-alone FKL can not achieve effective training when computed over only a small subset of the vocabulary. This is mainly because top-k FKL constitutes a biased approximation of the full-vocabulary FKL objective, and applying this biased divergence to all sampled tokens can introduce increasingly distorted policy gradients. Therefore, FKL is not suitable as the standalone objective for OPD under constrained vocabulary, while there would be great potential to use FKL enhancing OPD objectives like JSD."
- Why FKL is safe *specifically on outliers* (§4.2): "because reverse KL estimated from student-sampled tokens may fail to provide reliable supervision under severe distributional mismatch, we instead compute the distillation signal from the teacher perspective" and the self-limiting property "when `sum_{v in V_T^k} pi_S(v) → 0`, we have `KL(pi_T ‖ pi_S) → 0`, such that the auxiliary outlier objective is suppressed and does not interfere with gradient in the trust region."
- Why masking beats clipping (§4.2, with Figure 3): "Mask Outlier directly eliminates the influence of unreliable gradients. Compared with OPD and Clip Outlier, it maintains higher policy entropy, thereby better preserving the exploration capability during OPD training. Moreover, by removing the gradients induced by outlier tokens during backpropagation, TrOPD achieves a lower gradient norm than OPD and Clip Outlier, leading to more stable optimization."

## Quotes

Every off-policy statement in the paper:

- Abstract: "3) Off-Policy Guidance: The student continues generation from teacher prefixes and uses forward KL to imitate off-policy guidance, encouraging on-policy exploration toward reliable regions."
- §1: "Conventional off-policy distillation (Kim & Rush, 2016) trains students to imitate outputs generated by strong teacher models. Since training relies on teacher-generated trajectories while inference follows student-generated ones, this paradigm suffers from exposure bias, especially in long-chain-of-thought reasoning."
- §1: "To further encourage the student to generate within teacher-verifiable regions, we introduce off-policy guidance, which performs imitation learning from teacher-generated trajectories."
- §2: "distillation for generative language models has primarily relied on sequence-level knowledge distillation (Kim & Rush, 2016), an off-policy approach that performs supervised fine-tuning on teacher-generated responses."
- §3.1: "Different from off-policy distillation trained from the teacher of generations (ToGs), On-policy distillation instead trains on student of generations (SoGs) to mitigate exposure bias."
- §4.3: "To guide the student model to follow the teacher's trajectory, we propose an off-policy trust region to provide offline constraints, as illustrated in Fig. 2."
- §4.3: "For the off-policy region, since samples are generated from the teacher, `x ~ pi_T`, we adopt the K1 estimator for the forward KL, `KL(pi_T ‖ pi_S) = log pi_S/pi_T`, which achieves O(n) memory complexity."
- §4.3: "Initially, the maximum off-policy trajectory length is set to the maximum training sequence length. During training, it is gradually annealed to zero using a cosine schedule, such that generation becomes fully on-policy by the end of training."
- §5.1: "Since the student has already undergone extensive off-policy distillation, we directly conduct OPD without additional SFT."
- §5.3: "Incorporating off-policy guidance further improves the average scores of the Clip, Mask, and FKL Outlier variants, confirming its complementary benefit."
- §6: "We further introduce off-policy guidance to encourage exploration toward teacher-supported trajectories."
- App. A (teacher's own RL, not OPD): "During RLVR, GRPO is employed with a group size of 16, together with masked importance sampling to improve consistency between training and inference."

## Not in the source

- Nothing about stale student rollouts, replay, asynchronous generation, multiple updates per rollout batch, or a current-vs-old student ratio. The staleness question is simply not asked.
- No ablation of the cosine anneal schedule for the teacher-prefix length `l` (no fixed-`l`, no linear, no no-anneal control), and no per-step `l` values; only `beta = 0.001` is given.
- No clipping threshold `tau` value for the Clip Outlier / REOPOLD baselines.
- The trust mask is stochastic (`M_x ~ Bernoulli(P_trust)`) but no seed, no variance analysis, and no deterministic-threshold comparison.
- No full-vocabulary RKL control (ruled out on memory grounds, §3.1/§3.2) — so "K1 vs full-vocab reverse KL" is never measured, only "K1 vs truncated FKL".
- No training curves beyond entropy and gradient norm (Figure 3), no seeds/error bars on Tables 1 and 3–6, no throughput or wall-clock numbers.
- Framework/codebase not named in the text (GitHub link only).
